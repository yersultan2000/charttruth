    Standards
    
  
    
    
    The AI reads; the math judges.
    The language model only transcribes numbers and visual cues. Every verdict comes from published, deterministic rules that anyone can check by hand.
    
    
    It refuses to guess.
    Below 60% extraction confidence, or for an unsupported chart type, ChartTruth says “unable to verify” rather than inventing an answer.
    
    
    It avoids false accusations.
    A zoomed axis on a line chart is often legitimate. Science and finance line charts get a neutral context label, not a misleading flag.
    
    
    “Misleading” describes the design, not the author.
    A flag means the visual encoding distorts the numbers. It never claims anyone meant to deceive.
    
    
    People stay in control.
    Every extracted value is editable, and a person can mark the data as verified.
    
    
    Nothing leaves your device by default.
    No backend, no tracking. An image goes to Google’s API only when you add your own key, and the key is never stored beyond this tab.
    
    
    What it can’t see.
    One data series at a time. It doesn’t catch cherry-picked date ranges, misleading captions, missing context or bad source data, and vision models can misread small text. Misviz is mostly English-language web charts.
    
    
    About the lie factor.
    ChartTruth uses the ratio form of Edward Tufte’s idea: how many times bigger the largest bar looks than the smallest, divided by how many times bigger it really is.
