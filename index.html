    L3
    How it works
    The AI reads. The math decides.
    
    
    
      
      
        
    1. AI
      Read
      Gemini or Claude turns the image into numbers and visual cues. “Both” mode compares two models.
      2. 
        
    2. Person
      Check
      Every number is editable, with undo. When the AI is unsure or the models disagree, ChartTruth waits for you.
      3. 
        
    3. Rules
      Decide
      Exaggeration (lie factor), tick spacing, slice sums, flipped axes, two axes, 3D, and a guardrail for honest zoomed charts.
      4. 
        
    4. Rules
      Redraw
      Zero baseline, even ticks, upright, flat, pies rescaled to 100%, with a caption and alt text.
      5. 
      
    
      
    Asking an AI directly
    • Answer can change between runs
    • Can’t show its working
    • Often flags any axis not at zero
    • Can’t be corrected
    
        
    ChartTruth
    • Same numbers, same verdict
    • Every flag shows its math
    • Guardrail protects honest science charts
    • Fix a number and the verdict updates

    L5
    Our rules
    A tool that calls things misleading has to hold itself to a higher bar.
    
    
    
      
    No guessing
    Below 60% confidence, or when two models disagree, it says “can’t verify yet”.
    
      
    No false alarms
    Zoomed science and finance line charts get a neutral note, not a misleading flag.
    
      
    Design, not people
    A flag describes the chart’s design. It never claims anyone meant to deceive.
    
      
    You’re in control
    Every value is editable, with undo, and you can mark the data as checked.
    
      
    Private
    No servers, no tracking. Images go to an AI only with your own key.
    
      
    Accessible
    Alt text for every correction, full keyboard support, reduced motion.
    
      
    Honest limits
    One data series at a time. It can’t catch cherry-picked dates, bad captions or bad source data.
    
      
    Open method
    The lie factor follows Edward Tufte’s idea. Rules, prompts and benchmark code are public.
